# Investment Banking Resume: The One-Page Template That Gets Interviews

It's 11:40 on a Sunday night and a sophomore I work with through WSG, call him D, sends me his investment banking resume twenty minutes before a JPMorgan application closes. Two pages. A paragraph-long objective at the top. A bullet that reads "Responsible for assisting the team with various financial analyses." He has a 3.8 GPA and a real internship, and none of it is visible in the first ten seconds of reading.

I'm Stephen Turban, founder of WSG. I went to Harvard, went through McKinsey's on-cycle recruiting, and now spend a good part of every week reading resumes from undergrads who want to get into banking. The pattern is always the same: the students who get interviews don't have better experiences than the students who don't. They have resumes that make a screener's job easy.

## How long should an investment banking resume be?

One page. Not one page plus a line, not one page with 0.3 inch margins. Screeners at Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and every elite boutique read hundreds of resumes per opening, and a second page signals that you can't prioritize, which is the single skill an analyst gets judged on every day. **A two-page investment banking resume gets cut before anyone reads the second page.**

## Rules the live article teaches (keep these consistent across the resume articles)

- One page. 10 to 11 point font, 0.5 to 0.75 inch margins, black text, no color, no icons, no photo, no objective, no "references available upon request."
- Education first for an undergrad. GPA on its own line, two decimals, out of 4.00. 3.5+: list it. 3.3 to 3.5: list it, add major GPA if higher. Below 3.3: leaving it off reads as "below 3.3"; explain the trajectory instead.
- Expected graduation date on the degree line.
- Experience bullets: verb, what, how, number. Past tense for finished roles, present for current. Never "responsible for," "assisted with," "helped." Two to three bullets per role, one to two lines each.
- Every bullet should survive five minutes of "walk me through this."
- Real numbers, rounded the way you'd say them. Never invent one.
- Cut to one page by ranking, not shrinking the font.
- Leadership carries the sophomore resume.
- Additional Information: finance tools actually used, languages with proficiency, two to five specific interests.
- Inconsistent formatting and a single typo are the most common reasons a strong resume gets cut.
- Says "say this / don't say that" style rewrites as: "Don't write: ... / Write: ..." callouts.
