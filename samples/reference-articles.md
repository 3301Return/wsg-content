# WSG Reference Articles (October 2026)

Stephen's calibration set, sent October 8, 2026 via Sanskar. Six published WSG articles he marked as good and two he marked as bad, with his notes. **These are the voice and shape to aim for.** They're a guide, not a template: an article doesn't need to copy their structure, but its title, opening, headers and bolds should feel like the good set and never like the bad set.

The quoted lines and Stephen's notes are what matter most. The structure notes under each are from a quick look at the live pages.

Repo copy: `samples/reference-articles.md`. Packaged copy: `.claude-skill/reference-articles.md`.

## The good set

### 1. Is Wall Street Prep Worth It? 4 Pros And Cons You Need To Know

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/is-wall-street-prep-worth-it-4-pros-and-cons-you-need-to-know

Opening:

> I get asked about Wall Street Prep a lot. Students want to know if it's worth the money, whether it'll actually help them get an offer, and whether there's something better they should be using instead. Here's my honest take: what it does well, what it doesn't, and how to think about whether it's the right investment for your specific situation.

What to take from it: plain first person used as framing, the reader's real questions listed in their own words, and a last sentence that says what the article covers. Three sentences.

### 2. How Hard Is It to Get Into Investment Banking? 6 Things That Actually Matter

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/how-hard-is-it-to-get-into-investment-banking-6-things-that-actually-matter

Stephen's note: good because of "the sign posting of the bullet list," e.g., "1. Your school matters but probably not in the way you think."

Opening:

> Investment banking is one of the most selective entry-level career paths in the world. At top firms, acceptance rates for summer analyst roles are often estimated at well under 2–3%, with thousands of applicants competing for a limited number of spots.

Numbered headings in the article (each is a short plain claim that gives the takeaway):

- 1. Your school matters but probably not in the way you think.
- 2. Your GPA is a filter, not a scorecard.
- 4. Technical skills are the price of admission, not the differentiator.
- 6. Timing is the silent killer.

Structure: about 1,700 words, one bolded sentence per section.

### 3. Top 10 Sophomore Insights Programs for IB

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/top-10-sophomore-insights-programs-for-ib

Stephen: "this one is excellent." Notice the bolding in the second sentence: it's a full sentence. The questions are clear. "Notice how SIMPLE the title is and direct."

Opening:

> Insight programs are one of the best ways into Bulge Bracket and Elite Boutique investment banking. **Students who get into one of these programs are roughly 5 to 10 times more likely to convert into a Summer Analyst offer than students who skip them and apply through the open online portal.**

Question headers:

- What is a sophomore insights program?
- Are sophomore insights programs only for diverse candidates?

Structure: about 3,000 words. Programs grouped under plain H2s (Bulge Bracket, Elite Boutique, Boutique and Diversity-Focused), each item with a bolded one-line thesis, a short paragraph, Eligibility / Deadline / Who they look for bullets, and a bolded takeaway. Ends with a short game plan.

### 5. How to Beat McKinsey Solve: A Step-by-Step Guide

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/how-to-beat-mckinsey-solve-a-step-by-step-guide

Stephen: "See how the first paragraph just directly answers the question 'What is McKinsey Solve.'"

Opening:

> McKinsey Solve is the gamified pre-interview assessment every McKinsey candidate has to pass before getting a case interview. Roughly 60-75 minutes long, two mini-games, and a pass/fail score that McKinsey has never published the exact threshold for.

Structure: about 1,900 words. Headers are plain or questions ("How long does McKinsey Solve take and is it timed?", "How is McKinsey Solve scored?"), a short FAQ ("Can I retake McKinsey Solve if I fail?", "Do BCG and Bain use Solve?").

## The bad set

### 7. The Ultimate Guide to the BCG Casey Chatbot Interview

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-the-bcg-casey-chatbot-interview

Stephen: the beginning is like the AI-generated ones. "It doesn't address the question, it is generic, and it is weirdly opinionated about what people get wrong without showing evidence."

Opening (bad):

> Most candidates walk into the BCG Casey chatbot interview expecting a casual screening step and walk out rattled. There is no interviewer to read, no chance to ask a clarifying question, and a clock that never stops. You are solving a real case against a text box, and roughly seventy percent of what it asks you is quantitative. That is the part that catches people. Casey is not a personality quiz dressed up as a game.

Stephen's better version: start with what it is, like the McKinsey Solve article.

> The BCG Casey chatbot interview is the first-round interview for BCG candidates before you go to a case round. The chatbot round...

### 8. Top 10 Consulting Exit Opportunities

https://www.wallstreetguide.net/post/top-10-consulting-exit-opportunities

Opening (bad, Stephen: "this is terrible"):

> Consulting's best-kept secret is that the job is a lobby, not a house. A LinkedIn analysis of 1,644 people who left MBB (McKinsey & Company, Boston Consulting Group (BCG), and Bain & Company) in late 2025 found them scattering across finance, software, retail, startups, and, most of all, other consulting firms, mostly at private companies, mostly within three years of joining.

Stephen's better version:

> The average tenure for consultants is two years. So where do consultants go after finishing their time? For the most part there are four main areas: private equity, tech, big business, and entrepreneurship.
>
> In this article, I lay out the main pathways for people after consulting. It's broad, but there are a number of areas that appear more often than others.

## The patterns, in one table

| Good set does this | Bad set does this |
| --- | --- |
| First sentence says what it is or answers the title | First sentence tells a story about "most candidates" |
| One fact per sentence, one bolded full sentence with a number | A stat crammed into one sentence with parentheses and three "mostly" clauses |
| Plain words a person would say | "a lobby, not a house," "a clock that never stops," "dressed up as a game" |
| Opinions come with evidence, or as plain advice | Opinions about what readers get wrong, with no evidence |
| Short intro, then the value | Five sentences of buildup before any information |
| Simple title with the count, topic and year | (Titles weren't the main complaint on the bad two) |
| Numbered headings that give the takeaway; questions phrased as a student types them | Clever or decorative headers |
| Light "I" as framing | (Drafts before October 7 used full bio and stories) |
| 1,700 to 3,000 words | AI drafts ran to about 35 pages |
