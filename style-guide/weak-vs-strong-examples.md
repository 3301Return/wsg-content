# Weak vs Strong WSG Drafts

Use these as calibration. Before submitting any draft, scan it against the failure modes in the weak example.

## Weak example (do not write like this)

> Investment banking is one of the most competitive career paths for college students today. Many students aspire to break into top firms like Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Evercore, but the process can often feel overwhelming and confusing. In this guide, we will walk you through everything you need to know about breaking into investment banking.
>
> One of the most important aspects of investment banking recruiting is networking. Networking allows students to connect with professionals in the industry, learn more about the role, and potentially receive referrals. It is important to start networking early and be consistent in your efforts. Many successful candidates attribute their success to strong networking.
>
> Another key factor is technical preparation. Students should make sure they are familiar with common interview questions, including accounting, valuation, and financial modeling concepts...

### Why this fails

- Generic language that could apply to any career path
- No specific numbers, names, or scenarios
- States the obvious ("networking is important") without explaining how, when, or what outcome to expect
- Reads like a summary of common knowledge, not advice
- Uses filler phrases ("In this guide, we will walk you through...")
- No insight a smart sophomore couldn't get from one Reddit thread

## Strong example (write like this)

> Most students assume breaking into investment banking requires a finance degree, a target school, and a father who golfs with a Goldman partner. None of that is true.
>
> Every year, students from large state schools, non-finance majors, and first-generation backgrounds land offers at Goldman Sachs, Evercore, and Morgan Stanley. They do it by understanding a process most of their peers never figure out.
>
> This guide breaks that process down into ten concrete steps, starting from wherever you are right now.

### Why this works

- Opens with the reader's assumption (not a definition)
- Names firms, not vague "top banks"
- Acknowledges who actually wins, with specifics ("non-finance majors, first-generation backgrounds")
- Creates tension, resolves it
- Ends with a clear promise of value
- Respects the reader's intelligence — no hand-holding

## The 4-question gut check

After every draft, ask:

1. Does this sound like real experience?
2. Would a smart junior find this useful?
3. Could another blog have written this?
4. If I cut this paragraph, would it matter?

If any answer is "no" / "no" / "yes" / "no" — rewrite.
