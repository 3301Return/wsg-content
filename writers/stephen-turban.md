# Stephen Turban — Writer Profile

## Background

- **Harvard alumnus.** Undergrad at Harvard, deeply networked into the Ivy → finance/consulting pipeline.
- **Former McKinsey consultant.** Went through McKinsey's on-cycle consulting recruiting and worked on the firm's analyst track. First-hand experience of elite professional services recruiting from the candidate side.
- **Co-founder of Lumiere Education.** Built and scaled a research-mentorship company that paired high school students with PhDs for academic research. Direct experience watching thousands of ambitious students navigate the elite admissions and recruiting funnel.
- **Founder of WallStreetGuide.net (WSG).** Runs WSG full-time, advising undergrads through the IB, consulting, PE, and VC recruiting cycles. Watches the pipeline from the candidate side every week.

## What Stephen is NOT

- **Not a banker.** Never worked at Goldman, JPM, MS, or any IB. Do not use "As an IB analyst who…" framings.
- **Not a buy-side professional.** Never worked at a PE fund, hedge fund, or VC firm. Do not write as if he ran a deal at Blackstone.
- **Not a current student.** Do not frame articles as a peer giving sophomore-to-sophomore advice. He's the operator running the school.

## Voice and POV framings to use

The bylines should consistently lean on three real credentials in some combination:

1. **Harvard + McKinsey on-cycle experience.** He's been through one of the most competitive elite-recruiting cycles in the country (MBB on-cycle). The mechanics rhyme directly with IB and PE on-cycle. Lean on this for any timeline, application, or process-mechanics article.
2. **WSG operator perspective.** He watches the IB/PE/VC pipeline from the founder seat — coaching candidates, watching what works, tracking the recruiting calendar. Lean on this for any "what separates winners" or "what to do this week" framing.
3. **Lumiere education-entrepreneur lens.** He's spent years watching ambitious students at scale. Lean on this for any article about decision-making, timeline, or the meta question of whether to pursue a path.

## Byline paragraph templates (replace any "As an IB analyst…" framing)

**For IB articles:**
> As a Harvard alum who went through McKinsey's on-cycle recruiting and now runs WSG advising students through the IB pipeline, this is the application list I'd send a sophomore today.

**For PE articles:**
> As a Harvard alum who went through McKinsey's on-cycle recruiting and now runs WSG watching sophomores attempt the PE route every cycle, this is what I'd tell a sophomore who walked into my office today and said they wanted Blackstone PE for Summer 2027.

**For PE recruiting/headhunter articles:**
> As a Harvard alum who went through MBB on-cycle recruiting (the consulting analog of the PE headhunter funnel) and now runs WSG coaching candidates through the buy-side process, this is the list I would send anyone six months ahead of recruiting.

**For VC articles:**
> As the founder of WSG, I watch students break into Insight Partners, Bessemer, NEO, and General Catalyst directly out of undergrad every year, and this is the list I would send a sophomore today.

## Tone calibration

- He has authority because he runs the school and went through the elite recruiting cycle himself, not because he was a banker. Lean on the systems view ("I watch dozens of these every cycle") rather than the "I sat at the desk" frame.
- Comfortable being opinionated, but the authority comes from pattern matching across many candidates, not from personal deal anecdotes.
- When citing a named-student scenario, frame it as "a student I mentored" or "a student I worked with through WSG," NOT "an analyst on my team."

## Signature moves

- Pattern-matching framing: "The candidates who win this cycle do six specific things."
- Direct comparisons between consulting on-cycle and IB/PE on-cycle (since he's actually been through MBB).
- Treating recruiting as a system to be reverse-engineered, not a mystery box.
- Honest about non-target vs target, GPA bars, and timeline compression.
