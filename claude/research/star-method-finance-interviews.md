# Fact sheet: The STAR Method for Finance Interviews

Compiled October 11, 2026 for WSG. UNVERIFIED means not confirmed on a primary page.

## 1. Origin and definition

- DDI's claim: "DDI invented the STAR method as one of the simplest and most effective ways to communicate" and "We're the pioneers—DDI introduced the STAR Method in 1974 with Targeted Selection." No outside confirmation of 1974. (Source: https://www.ddi.com/solutions/behavioral-interviewing/star-method, Oct 2026)
- DDI's letters: S/T "Explain the situation or task so others understand the context." A "Give details about what you or another person did to handle the situation." R "Describe what was achieved by the action and why it was effective." DDI folds Situation and Task into one "ST" step. (Source: same URL, Oct 2026)
- Targeted Selection page: "Targeted Selection is DDI's proven behavioral interviewing system." (Source: https://www.ddi.com/solutions/targeted-selection, Oct 2026)
- Founder William Byham (2020): "Since we introduced Targeted Selection® in the early 1970s"; behavioral interviewing "which I invented and DDI brought to market." (Source: https://www.ddi.com/blog/10-insights-from-50-years-of-innovation, Oct 2026)
- Tom Janz "introduces this approach at a conference in 1977, but only addresses it in print in his 1982 article," cited as "Initial Comparisons of Patterned Behavior Description Interviews versus Unstructured Interviews," Journal of Applied Psychology 67(5): 577-580. Secondary source; PsycNet blocked. (Source: https://campanthropology.org/2018/09/07/origins-of-job-application-forms/, Oct 2026)
- Variants, exact letters:
  - STAR = Situation, Task, Action, Result. (Source: https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/careers/join-deloitte/recruiting-tips/tips-to-prepare-for-an-interview.html, Oct 2026)
  - STARR / STAR(R) = Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflect ("optional": "share what you learned, what you might do differently next time"). CAR = Challenge, Action, Result. (Source: https://governmentdigitalanddatahub.campaign.gov.uk/join/government-digital-and-data-careers/prepare-for-your-interview/starr-and-car-techniques, Oct 2026). University of Reading: "an additional R can be added to show what you learned from the experience." (Source: https://www.reading.ac.uk/essentials/-/media/essentials/files/careers/careers-help-and-support-guides/applications-interviews-and-cvs/careers-star-technique-for-applications-and-interviews.pdf, Oct 2026)
  - CAR as Context, Action, Result and SHER = Situation, Hurdle, Endgame, Reflection (cited to "Murphy, 2023"), both in JPMorganChase's handbook: "Context: What was the challenge? Action: What did you do? Result: What was the outcome?" (Source: https://www.jpmorganchase.com/content/dam/jpmorganchase/documents/careers/jpmc-digital-handbook.pdf, Oct 2026)
  - SOAR = Situation, Obstacle, Action, Result. (Source: https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/the-star-method/, Oct 2026)
  - SPSIL = Situation, Problem, Solution, Impact, Lessons; that page says STAR's weak spots are separating Task from Action and omitting lessons learned. (Source: https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/investment-banking-behavioral-fit-interview-questions, Oct 2026)
  - PAR = Problem, Action, Result and STARL = Situation, Task, Action, Result, Learning: UNVERIFIED.

## 2. Evidence on structured and behavioral interview validity

- Schmidt and Hunter (1998), Psychological Bulletin 124(2), 262-274: structured interviews 0.51, unstructured 0.38, GMA 0.51, work samples 0.54. (Source: https://master-hr.com/insights/new-study-providing-updated-validity-estimates/, Oct 2026; same figures at https://www.testpartnership.com/academy/structured-interview.html, Oct 2026)
- Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens (2022), Journal of Applied Psychology 107, 2040-2068: structured 0.42 (SD 0.19), unstructured 0.19 (SD 0.16), GMA 0.31, work samples 0.33. (Source: master-hr table above, Oct 2026; citation per https://www.siop.org/tip-article/is-cognitive-ability-the-best-predictor-of-job-performance, Oct 2026)
- Open-access companion, Sackett et al. (2023), Industrial and Organizational Psychology 16(3), 283-300: "Structured interviews, which top our list in terms of a mean validity of .42," 80% credibility interval .18 to .66; revised estimates were ".19 lower for unstructured interviews." (Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/industrial-and-organizational-psychology/article/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D, Oct 2026)
- 2022 ranking: structured interviews .42, job knowledge tests .40, biodata .38, work samples .33, cognitive ability .31. (Source: SIOP URL above, Oct 2026)
- A 2016 re-analysis (Schmidt, Oh and Shaffer) is described as overcorrecting for range restriction; its numbers UNVERIFIED. (Source: Test Partnership URL above, Oct 2026)

## 3. What the firms themselves say

Official pages that name STAR:

- Deloitte US: "The STAR method is a widely used framework that can help you organize and present stories to demonstrate your experience." "STAR stands for 'situation, task, action, and result.'" Also "What are some quantitative results you can point to?" and "it's important not to make stories up." (Source: https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/careers/join-deloitte/recruiting-tips/tips-to-prepare-for-an-interview.html, Oct 2026)
- Deloitte Australia recruiters: "At Deloitte, we use behavioural interviewing." "There's a simple framework that can help you stay clear, confident and focused: the STAR technique." "Even in a team example, we're assessing your individual capability." Samples: "Tell me about a time you worked in a team." "Give an example of when you showed leadership." (Source: https://www.deloitte.com/au/en/careers/deloitte-life/blogs/recruiter-interview-tips-student.html, Oct 2026)
- PwC Canada campus: "Get ready to answer behavioural questions and to share examples using the STAR method (situation, task, action, result)." (Source: https://www.pwc.com/ca/en/careers/campus/applying-to-pwc.html, Oct 2026)
- Bank of America, bylined "By Bank of America" on Bright Network (third-party host): STAR "stops us from waffling or getting lost when answering an interview question." (Source: https://www.brightnetwork.co.uk/employer-advice/assessment-centre-advice-bank-of-america/, Oct 2026)

Official pages on behavioral questions without STAR:

- JPMorganChase Digital Handbook (©2025): "1. Virtual assessment: First round recorded virtual interview hosted over HireVue and/or HackerRank (technology only)." "You can use the SHER model to help structure your answer." Don'ts: "Use examples that are not from your own experience"; "Go off on tangents, unrelated to the question – keep your answers concise." (Source: handbook PDF above, Oct 2026)
- Goldman Sachs Prepare: "If selected following your HireVue interview, you'll be invited to a series of final round interviews." "Typically, we conduct between two and five interviews for campus hires, depending on the division." (Source: https://www.goldmansachs.com/careers/students/prepare, Oct 2026)
- Morgan Stanley: "Interviews could be competency, strengths based, skills based or technical." "Avoid sounding too rehearsed." (Source: https://www.morganstanley.com/people-opportunities/students-graduates/resources/interview-preparation, Oct 2026)
- McKinsey: prepare "two personal examples that demonstrate how you've made an impact in each of the four areas," "focusing on your specific role and describing the key actions that were critical to success." (Source: https://www.mckinsey.com/careers/interviewing, Oct 2026)
- Bain: "we provide all candidates the same questions for each role to reduce bias"; "We want people who are intellectually curious, entrepreneurial, and who find challenges exciting." (Source: https://www.bain.com/careers/hiring-process/interviewing/, Oct 2026). Feedback is "based on an objective scoring system." (Source: https://www.bain.com/careers/interview-prep/, Oct 2026)
- Not found or unreachable: Citi (old interview-advice pages redirect to jobs.citi.com), Barclays, UBS, Deutsche Bank, Evercore, Blackstone, KKR, BCG (careers.bcg.com pages errored). UNVERIFIED whether any mention STAR.

## 4. HireVue timing facts

- Goldman official: first interview is "a video interview, which will take approximately 30 minutes to complete." No question count or retake policy. (Source: Goldman Prepare URL in Section 3, Oct 2026)
- Goldman per eFinancialCareers (July 2018): "five questions and possibly a sixth if you've applied to multiple divisions"; "You'll have 30 seconds to prepare after hearing the question"; "the recording will shut down right at two minutes"; "It's one-and-done with Goldman"; recruiters say answers are not reviewed "by robots or anything like that." (Source: https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2018/07/video-interview-goldman-sachs, Oct 2026)
- Goldman per IGotAnOffer (Sept 2026): "3 to 6 questions," "30 seconds of prep time per prompt," "1:30 to 2 minutes to respond," "1 to 3 retries per question," about a week to complete. (Source: https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/goldman-sachs-hirevue-interview, Oct 2026)
- Goldman per RoadToOffer (Sept 2026): "Around 3" questions; retakes "Conflicting reports (0 to 3); no confirmed universal rule." (Source: https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/goldman-sachs-hirevue, Oct 2026)
- JPMorgan Summer Analyst per IGotAnOffer (Sept 2026): "3-5 questions," "30-120 seconds of prep time," "1.5-2 minutes to record each answer," "1 optional retry for each question." (Source: https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/jpm-hirevue-interview, Oct 2026)
- JPMorgan Summer Analyst, Glassdoor (London, Sept 2026): "hirevue interview with 3 questions"; "You get one retake, and 30 seconds to prepare for each question." (Source: https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/J-P-Morgan-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE145.0,10_KO11,25.htm, Oct 2026)
- Morgan Stanley Summer Analyst, Glassdoor (Sept 2026): "HireVue interview required with 3 questions same for every year": "Why MS, Why team and Tech trend in the next 6-12 months." - HireVue's own pages: "Most on-demand interviews ask you to give your answer in just a few minutes." "You'll even have the opportunity to do a practice question." (Source: https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/interview-tips, Oct 2026). "Hiring decisions are made by people." (Source: https://www.hirevue.com/candidates/faq, Oct 2026)
- Mergers & Inquisitions: "3-5 questions," 30 seconds prep, 90-second answers; "In a 90-second response, the max should be 180-190 words (ideally closer to 150)." (Source: https://mergersandinquisitions.com/hirevue-interview/, Oct 2026)
- Answer length: "Keep answers under two minutes." (Source: https://careers.rhsmith.umd.edu/blog/2026/02/02/investment-banking-interview-prep-2026-guide/, Oct 2026). "Keep each of your answers within 2 minutes." (Source: IGotAnOffer behavioral URL, Oct 2026)

## 5. Common behavioral questions at banks

IGO = https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/investment-banking-behavioral-fit-interview-questions; WSO = https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/resources/interviews/behavioral-interview-questions; RTO = https://www.roadtooffer.com/blog/investment-banking-behavioral-interview-questions (all Oct 2026). Bank labels follow IGO's per-bank sections.

1. "Tell me about a time you failed and how you overcame it." (Goldman, IGO); "Tell me about a time when you failed" (Deutsche Bank, https://targetjobs.co.uk/employers/deutsche-bank/how-to-approach-interview-questions-at-deutsche-bank-323035, Oct 2026)
2. "Tell me about a time you worked in a team." (JPM and MS, IGO)
3. "Tell me about a time you handled team conflict. How did you solve it?" (IGO); "Tell us about a time you faced a disagreement in a group." (JPM, IGO)
4. "Tell me about a time when you demonstrated leadership." (Goldman, IGO)
5. "Tell me about a time when you worked on a project with a tight deadline." (Goldman, IGO)
6. "Tell me about a time you used data to make a recommendation." (MS, IGO); "Tell us about a time you had to collect and analyze data" (JPM HireVue, https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/jpm-hirevue-interview, Oct 2026)
7. "Tell me about a time when you were persuasive." (MS, IGO); "Tell me about a time you influenced without authority." (RTO)
8. "Please tell me a time when you receive feedback (both positive and negative)." (WSO); "Tell me about difficult feedback you received." (RTO)
9. "Tell me about a time you had to learn quickly." (WSO)
10. "Tell me about a time when a team member wasn't pulling their weight in a project. What did you do?" (Goldman, IGO)
11. "Tell me about a time your manager questioned your decision." (Goldman, IGO)
12. "Tell me about a time you had to work with somebody you disliked." (MS, IGO)
13. "Tell me about a time when you encountered a difficult client and describe how you handled the situation." (JPM, IGO)
14. "Tell me about a time you showed resilience." (JPM, IGO)
15. "Tell me about a time you did the right thing." (MS, IGO)
16. "Tell me about a time you took initiative or ownership of a task." (Goldman, IGO)
17. "Tell me about a time when you made a mistake. What did you do to resolve it?" (WSO)
18. "Tell me about a time when you had too many things to do and had to prioritize your tasks." (Source: https://prod.uc.edu/campus-life/careereducation/career-studio/job-search/interviews/star-method, Oct 2026)
- Caveat: Glassdoor's Goldman Summer Analyst page showed mostly resume and markets prompts. (Source: https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Goldman-Sachs-Summer-Analyst-Interview-Questions-EI_IE2800.0,13_KO14,28.htm, Oct 2026)

## 8. Common mistakes documented by career offices

- MIT CAPD: Situation 20%, Task 10%, Action 60%, Result 10%; "they do not need to know every detail!"; "using 'we' statements can make it difficult for an employer to have a clear understanding of what your skills are"; "It is important to highlight quantifiable results"; memorized scripts "may limit your ability to adapt"; prepare 3-5 stories. (Source: https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews, Oct 2026)
- University of Cincinnati: "You must describe a specific event or situation, not a general description of what you have done in the past." Use "I," not "we." Include results "without being asked." (Source: UC URL in Section 5, Oct 2026)
- DePauw: "Oftentimes students have to be prompted to include their results, so try to include that without being asked." "Be sure the outcome or result reflects positively on you (even if the result itself was not favorable)." (Source: https://www.depauw.edu/files/resources/star-method.pdf, Oct 2026)
- UMD Smith (Feb 2026): same 20/10/60/10 split; mistakes: rambling, generic "Why IB," negative framing of past employers, over-rehearsed delivery, no prepared questions. (Source: UMD URL in Section 4, Oct 2026)
- Employer side: Deloitte AU "short, structured and punchy is always better than long and vague"; JPMorganChase "keep your answers concise." (Sources in Section 3, Oct 2026)
