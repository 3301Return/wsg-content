# Fact sheet: Credit Analyst Interview Questions: 20 to Prepare For

Compiled October 11, 2026. Every source was viewed in October 2026; the date in parentheses is the source's own publication or data date. Some targets (Glassdoor, Fitch's site, Investopedia, several OCC pages) could not be reached; gaps are listed under "Unverified or conflicting."

## 1. Market numbers for context

- US leveraged loan market: Morningstar LSTA US Leveraged Loan Index outstandings hit "a record $1.55 trillion by year-end" 2025. (Source: https://www.lsta.org/content/morningstar-lsta-leveraged-loan-index-analysis-december-2025/, Jan 2026)
- Corroboration: "$1.5 trillion par outstanding," data as of 12/31/2025. (Source: https://loomissayles.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Loomis-on-Loans-Q4-2025-Final.pdf, Jan 2026)
- Maturity wall: about $353 billion of index loans mature within three years, 23% of outstanding, per PitchBook LCD data through December 2025. (Source: https://www.kirkland.com/-/media/content/bring-down/kirkland1/1-22-26/finjan26.pdf, Jan 2026)
- Cov-lite share: 91% of outstanding US leveraged loans ($1.29 trillion) at year-end 2024 and 93% of 2024 institutional new issuance, per an unsourced prep page. UNVERIFIED until matched to PitchBook LCD. (Source: https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/debt-capital-markets/cov-lite-loans-the-maintenance-covenant-shift, 2026). Anchor: cov-lite passed 90% of issuance in 2021. (Source: https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2021/10/covenant-lite-deals-exceed-90-of-leveraged-loan-issuance-setting-new-high-66935148, Oct 2021)
- Private credit AUM: Moody's expects AUM to exceed $2 trillion in 2026 and approach $4 trillion by 2030. (Source: https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/insights/credit-risk/outlooks/private-credit-2026.html, Jan 21, 2026). Preqin forecasts $4.5 trillion by 2030; its current measured figure is paywalled. (Source: https://www.preqin.com/c/key-findings-global-report-2026-italy, Dec 2025). Preqin private debt AUM was about $1.7 trillion as of June 2023 (Kirkland PDF above).
- Default rates (Fitch, trailing 12 months, July 2026): leveraged loans about 3.8%, high-yield bonds 2.7%, private credit about 6.1%. Fitch's year-end 2026 forecasts: loans 4.5% to 5.0%, high-yield 2.5% to 3.0%. Secondary summary of Fitch's monitor. (Source: https://cryptobriefing.com/fitch-us-corporate-default-rates-july-2026/, Aug 17, 2026)
- Fitch private credit default rate: 6.3% through August 2026, a record; issuers under $25 million EBITDA at 12.0%. (Source: https://www.webull.com/news/15578827387765760, Benzinga via Webull, Sep 14, 2026)
- Recovery rates (Moody's, long run): first-lien bank loans 65% on a trading-price basis, 1983 to 2023, versus 38% for senior unsecured bonds. Ultimate recoveries, 1987 to 2023: term loans 71%, senior unsecured bonds 47%. Annual first-lien recoveries ranged from about 53% to 88% over 1990 to 2023. (Source: https://cdn.bma.bm/documents/2024-09-05-11-50-00-Private-Credit---Deep-dive-on-Direct-Loans-CLOs-and-Private-Placements.pdf, citing Moody's, Sep 2024)
- LBO leverage (KBRA DLD, December 2025): upper middle market and large-cap LBOs 5.5x total debt/EBITDA, core middle market 4.75x, lower middle market 4.1x. (Source: Kirkland PDF above, citing KBRA DLD, Jan 15, 2026)
- LBO purchase multiples: 11.1x EBITDA for syndicated-loan-financed LBOs in 2025, up from 11.0x. (Source: https://www.opalesque.com/712921/Green_shoots_emerge_in_private_equity_but_exit292.html, Jan 2, 2026)
- Private credit financed 214 LBOs in 2025, 84% by count; the syndicated market financed 42% by volume. (Source: Kirkland PDF above, citing PitchBook)
- Direct lending pricing: unitranche loans clustered at SOFR + 450 to 500 bps in October 2025; 45% of LCD-tracked buyout financings priced in that band in 2025 versus 18% in 2024. (Source: https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/October_2025_US_Private_Credit_Monitor.pdf, Oct 2025)
- Maintenance covenants in direct lending: 77% of core middle market loans originated in 2024 had them, versus 38% of large-cap (jumbo) private loans. (Source: https://www.futurestandard.com/insights/chart-of-the-week/lender-protections-lower-core-middle-market, citing KBRA DLD, Mar 27, 2026)
- Allvue sample of 1,933 private credit issuances: loans with a leverage-only maintenance covenant fell from 74.2% in 2022 to 40.9% in 1H 2025. (Source: https://www.allvuesystems.com/allvue-private-credit-monitor/, May 2026)
- Covenant cushions: historically about 25% EBITDA headroom; now almost all deals 30% or more. (Source: https://www.proskauer.com/alert/private-credit-deep-dives-leverage-covenants-and-auto-resets, Jun 2023)

## 2. Rating scales and definitions

- Moody's scale, quoted: Aaa "highest quality, with minimal credit risk"; Baa "subject to moderate credit risk ... medium-grade and as such may possess certain speculative characteristics"; Ba "speculative elements and are subject to substantial credit risk"; B "speculative and are subject to high credit risk"; Caa "poor standing and are subject to very high credit risk"; C "the lowest rated class of bonds and are typically in default." (Source: https://www.moodys.com/sites/products/productattachments/moodys%20rating%20symbols%20and%20definitions.pdf, undated)
- Moody's outlook: "an opinion regarding the likely direction of an issuer's rating over the medium term," with Positive, Negative, Stable and Developing categories. Watchlist: "Moody's uses the Watchlist to indicate that a rating is under review for possible change in the short-term." (Same source)
- Moody's LGD assessments: LGD1 0% to under 10%; LGD2 10% to under 30%; LGD3 30% to under 50%; LGD4 50% to under 70%; LGD5 70% to under 90%; LGD6 90% to 100%. (Same source)
- S&P investment-grade line: "Investment grade ratings from S&P Global Ratings include those rated BBB- or higher." (Source: https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/about/understanding-credit-ratings, 2026)
- S&P issue definitions, quoted: BBB "exhibits adequate protection parameters"; BB "is less vulnerable to nonpayment than other speculative issues"; B "is more vulnerable to nonpayment than obligations rated 'BB'"; CCC "is currently vulnerable to nonpayment"; D "is in default or in breach of an imputed promise." "Obligations rated 'BB', 'B', 'CCC', 'CC', and 'C' are regarded as having significant speculative characteristics." Notching: "Junior obligations are typically rated lower than senior obligations, to reflect lower priority in bankruptcy." (Source: S&P Global Ratings Definitions, Oct 2018, mirrored at https://www.idl.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/rulemaking/20.03.02-2019/research/Standard__Poors_Ratings.pdf)
- S&P outlook and CreditWatch, quoted: an outlook covers "the intermediate term (typically six months to two years)"; CreditWatch applies when "there is at least a one-in-two likelihood of a rating change within 90 days." (Source: S&P "General Criteria: Use Of CreditWatch And Outlooks," 2009, republished 2016, mirrored at https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/RS20171120132502.pdf)
- NRSRO market share of outstanding ratings, Dec 31, 2023: S&P 49.96%, Moody's 31.73%, Fitch 12.46%, combined 94.15%. (Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/jan-2025-ocr-staff-report.pdf, Jan 2025). The SEC's statistics page now holds Dec 31, 2025 data in a spreadsheet, not retrieved.
## 3. Bank credit analyst machinery

- Five Cs: "the important general characteristics that lenders should consider and evaluate": Character, Capacity, Capital, Collateral, Conditions. (Source: https://www.sfnet.com/home/education/glossary-detail/5-c's-of-credit, Secured Finance Network). Wells Fargo's own page substitutes "credit history" for character. (Source: https://www.wellsfargo.com/financial-education/credit-management/five-c/)
- DSCR minimum: "Most commercial banks and equipment finance firms want to see a minimum of 1.25x," with covenants often set at "not less than 1.25x." (Source: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/commercial-lending/debt-service-coverage-ratio)
- SBA 7(a): SOP 50 10 8.1 raises the DSCR floor from 1.15x to 1.25x effective Oct 1, 2026. The ratios come from a practitioner post; the date and SOP number are confirmed by Doeren Mayhew. (Sources: https://searchfunder.com/post/on-oct-1-the-sbas-dscr-floor-moves-to-125x-does-your-deal-still-clear, 2026; https://www.doeren.com/viewpoint/sop-50-10-8-1-what-sba-lenders-should-be-thinking-about-before-oct-1, Aug 24, 2026)
- Leveraged lending guidance: the 2013 Interagency Guidance set no bright line, but the 2014 FAQs were read as treating total debt above 6.0x EBITDA as raising concern, and expected borrowers to show capacity to repay about 50% of debt in 5 to 7 years. The OCC and FDIC rescinded both on Dec 5, 2025; the Fed had not yet said whether it would follow. (Source: https://winston.com/en/insights-news/occ-and-fdic-rescind-interagency-leveraged-lending-guidance, Dec 12, 2025)
- Fixed-charge coverage ratio, textbook form: "((Earnings before interest and taxes) + Lease expense) / (Interest expense + Lease expense)." (Source: https://www.accountingtools.com/articles/fixed-charge-coverage-ratio). The lender form (EBITDA less capex, cash taxes and distributions, over cash interest plus scheduled principal) was not captured as a quote: UNVERIFIED.
- Asset-based lending: the OCC's Asset-Based Lending handbook (Mar 27, 2014) describes receivable advance rates of 70% to 85%, up to 95% in some cases, before dilution and reserves; inventory advance rates up to 65% on liquidation value; and expects "a typical borrowing base to be comprised of accounts receivable and inventory." (Source: https://www.abfjournal.com/?p=28426, ABF Journal summary; booklet announced in OCC Bulletin 2014-11, https://occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2014/bulletin-2014-11.html)
- Expected loss: "Expected Loss = PD x LGD x EAD." PD "measures the likelihood that a borrower will default on their obligations within a specific timeframe"; LGD "represents the percentage of exposure a lender expects to lose if a default occurs"; EAD "quantifies the total value exposed to loss at the time of default." (Source: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/commercial-lending/expected-loss/, Jan 2025)
- Regulatory classifications, quoted from the FDIC Manual of Examination Policies: "A Special Mention asset has potential weaknesses that deserve management's close attention." "Substandard loans are inadequately protected by the current sound worth and paying capacity of the obligor." "Loans classified Loss are considered uncollectible and of such little value" that carrying them is not warranted. (Source: https://www.fdic.gov/laws-and-regulations/appeals-material-supervisory-determinations-guidelines-decisions-44, FDIC SARC-2004-01). Doubtful "has all the weaknesses inherent in one classified Substandard," with full collection "highly questionable and improbable." (Source: https://fhfa.gov/advisory-bulletin/ab-2021-03, Aug 2021, same uniform wording)
- The OCC's "Rating Credit Risk" booklet (April 2001) governs bank rating systems; regulators require the "four criticized categories: special mention, substandard, doubtful and loss," with higher grades as pass credits. (Sources: https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications/publications-by-type/comptrollers-handbook/rating-credit-risk/pub-ch-rating-credit-risk.pdf; https://www.abrigo.com/?p=4709, Feb 2015)
- Credit memo contents: no OCC or bank description retrieved. See section 4, downside case, for what the analyst's model holds.

## 4. Leveraged finance machinery

- Capital structure order: senior debt (revolver and term loans) "is paid out first"; subordinated debt next, within which high-yield bonds rank above mezzanine, PIK notes and vendor notes; equity gets what remains "after all creditors are paid." (Source: https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/commercial-lending/senior-and-subordinated-debt/, Mar 2020, modified Dec 2025)
- First vs second lien, TLA vs TLB: "the claims on collateral of second-lien loans are junior to those of first-lien loans," with a pricing premium that "typically starts at 200 bps." TLA is "a term loan with a progressive repayment schedule that typically runs six years or less," sold to banks. TLB is "a term loan facility carved out for nonbank, institutional accounts," traditionally bullet. (Source: https://pitchbook.com/leveraged-commentary-data/leveraged-loan-primer, PitchBook LCD, updated Mar 4, 2026)
- Pricing convention: spreads quoted over a base rate, as in "Sofr+250." A 3% SOFR floor means resets use 3% if SOFR falls below it. A loan issued at 99 has an OID of 100 bps, or one point. Price flex lets arrangers move pricing on demand. Repricings follow "a deal's six-month call premium." Leveraged loan definition: rated BB+ or lower, or spread of at least 125 bps. (Same primer)
- Loan vs high-yield bond: loans are floating-rate, usually secured, amortize with mandatory prepayments, and carry at most a six-month soft call on TLBs; bonds are usually unsecured, bullet, with 2 to 5 year non-call periods (NC2, NC3, NC5) then declining premiums, and incurrence covenants only. TLBs have no maintenance covenants "apart from a springing covenant for the revolver." (Source: https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/-/media/ashurst/documents/news-and-insights/insights/2018/apr/high-yield-bonds-and-leveraged-loans.pdf, Ashurst, 2018)
- Covenant definitions, quoted: cov-lite loans are "loans that have bond-like financial incurrence covenants, rather than traditional maintenance covenants." Maintenance covenants "require an issuer to meet certain financial tests every quarter, whether or not it takes an action." Incurrence tests apply only when the issuer acts, such as paying a dividend or adding debt. (LCD primer)
- Unitranche: "structured as a single financing arrangement comprised of a roll-up of separate tranches"; the agreement among lenders sets the first-out and last-out waterfall; the blended rate sits at or above senior pricing and below second-lien pricing. (Source: https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/unitranche-debt/, Jan 2024)
- PIK: CFI lists "Payment in Kind (PIK) notes" among subordinated instruments below high-yield bonds and mezzanine (CFI page above). A quotable definition was not retrieved: UNVERIFIED as a quote.
- Downside case: private credit cases focus on "downside cases" and credit stats such as FCCR and DSCR; on-site cases run 90 minutes to 3 to 4 hours with projections, a free cash flow bridge and a simple debt schedule. (Sources: https://mergersandinquisitions.com/private-credit/; https://mergersandinquisitions.com/direct-lending/)
## 5. Interview process and pay by seat

- JPMorgan MMBSI Summer Analyst: 9 weeks, opening with a week of training in client management, credit structuring and cash management; interns build financing models and work with credit officers; strong interns get offers for the 2.5-year full-time MMBSI Analyst Program. Process: application, then a recorded HireVue interview. Preferred GPA 3.2+. (Source: https://builtin.com/job/2026-commercial-and-investment-banking-middle-market-banking-specialized-industries-summer, removed Jan 2025)
- Commercial bank credit roles: "Interviews are rarely technical"; expect fit plus basic accounting and credit. (Source: https://mergersandinquisitions.com/credit-analyst/, data "as of 2022")
- S&P Global Ratings CREDit Graduate Program: two years, next intake September 2026; year one in a home practice, year two exposure to other practices; analysts pursue the CFA or similar; all majors; at least one in-person interview. (Source: https://builtin.com/job/credit-graduate-program-2026/7667314, removed Nov 2025)
- Moody's Ratings Associate: "in-depth analysis of a select credit ratings portfolio," working with Lead Analysts, joining rating committees and meeting issuers; rolling openings. (Source: https://careers.moodys.com/en/early-careers)
- LevFin: recruiting mirrors other IB groups with more technical and fewer macro questions; analyst and associate pay is "similar to compensation in any other group." (Source: https://mergersandinquisitions.com/leveraged-finance/)
- Private credit: two case formats, a quick CIM read with a decision, or 3 to 4 hour cases with model and presentation; "Most private credit case studies are like 3-statement models or LBO models, but with simpler mechanics." (M&I private credit)
- Pay: commercial bank credit analyst $60K to $80K total to start, bonus 5% to 10% of base (M&I, 2022); corporate banking analysts $95K to $110K base, $120K to $165K total (https://mergersandinquisitions.com/corporate-banking/, "as of 2025"); rating agency "$100K+ all-in" to start, associates near IB analyst pay with much lower bonuses (M&I credit analyst, 2022); direct lending analyst $90K to $140K total, associate $125K to $250K, a 10% to 20% discount to IB, undated (M&I direct lending).
- BLS benchmark: financial analysts median $103,570 (May 2025); the Credit Analysts OES page was unreachable. (Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/financial-analysts.htm)

## 6. Most-asked credit interview questions (30, with source)

CFI = https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/career/credit-analyst-interview-questions/ (2018, modified May 2026). PC = https://mergersandinquisitions.com/private-credit/. DL = https://mergersandinquisitions.com/direct-lending/. CB = https://mergersandinquisitions.com/corporate-banking/. IBIQ = https://ibinterviewquestions.com/guides/debt-capital-markets/major-direct-lenders-apollo-ares-blackstone-blue-owl-hps (May 2026).

1. "Walk me through your resume." (PC, DL, CB)
2. "Why private credit rather than private equity or hedge funds?" (PC); "Why direct lending rather than private equity or mezzanine?" (DL)
3. "Why corporate banking rather than investment banking?" (CB)
4. "What do direct lenders do?" (DL)
5. "What are the 5 C's of Credit, and how are they used?" (CFI)
6. "How would you evaluate the creditworthiness of a company?" (CB)
7. "What considerations would you make to decide whether to lend $10 million to a new corporate borrower?" (CFI)
8. "What are the credit metrics that banks look at when extending credit?" (CFI)
9. "Is there a specific debt-to-capital ratio that banks target?" (CFI)
10. "Walk me through the 3 financial statements and how you link them." (CB)
11. "What is EBITDA, and how do you calculate it starting with Net Income or Operating Income?" (CB)
12. "Walk me through how to calculate FCF from EBITDA without a full 3-statement model." (PC)
13. "Is Free Cash Flow (FCF) the best metric for evaluating a company's ability to service its Debt? If not, what's better?" (PC)
14. "What is the interest coverage ratio?" (CFI)
15. "How do you calculate the FCCR and DSCR, and what do they mean?" (DL)
16. "What are maintenance and incurrence covenants?" (CB)
17. "Are maintenance or incurrence covenants more common in private credit? Why?" (PC)
18. "What are the key maintenance covenants you would analyze in a credit deal?" (DL)
19. Covenant cushion: EBITDA $100, Debt $500, 6% cost of debt; find the cushion against a 6x maximum leverage and 2x minimum coverage test. (CB)
20. "What's the difference between Revolvers and Term Loans?" (CB)
21. "What is original issue discount (OID)?" (CB)
22. "What's the difference between the Yield to Maturity, Yield to Call, and Yield to Worst?" (PC)
23. "How can you quickly approximate the yield to maturity on a bond?" (DL)
24. Loan IRR: a $200 million TLB, 7-year maturity, 1% annual amortization, benchmark falling from 5% to 3%; approximate the IRR. (PC)
25. A sponsor wants 7x rather than 5x Debt/EBITDA on an LBO; how would you evaluate the claim? (PC)
26. Two TLB borrowers with different FCCRs; does the higher FCCR necessarily mean less credit risk? (PC)
27. "What is a 'workout,' and how does it differ from a formal restructuring?" (PC)
28. "What are the main differences between private credit (PC) and 'broadly syndicated loans' (BSL)?" (PC)
29. "Why has private credit grown so fast?" (IBIQ)
30. "What do the credit rating agencies do?" and "What are the current SOFR and Treasury rates?" (CFI)

## 7. Real public examples

- Warner Bros. Discovery, 2025: S&P cut WBD to junk on May 20, 2025 (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-20/warner-bros-discovery-downgraded-to-junk-by-s-p). Fitch cut the IDR from BBB- to BB+ on June 11, 2025, Negative Watch, calling the post-split company "smaller and less diversified in a secularly declining industry"; WarnerMedia Holdings notes BB+ (RR4), WarnerMedia notes BB (RR5). Moody's cut the senior unsecured notes from Baa3 to Ba1, on review for downgrade, citing "WBD's persistent operating challenges and a change in its financial policies" and "the declining linear television business." Gross debt about $37 billion. (Source: https://www.thewrap.com/fitch-moodys-warner-bros-discovery-credit-ratings-downgrade/, Jun 12, 2025)
- Adecco Group, Nov 17, 2025: S&P cut it from BBB+ to BBB, outlook to stable, citing "persistently high leverage," expected at about 2.0x versus 1.6x previously. (Source: https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/the-adecco-group-responds-to-sp-global-ratings-downgrade, Nov 2025)
- Sovereign: Moody's cut the United States from Aaa to Aa1 on May 16, 2025, outlook stable, citing the "increase over more than a decade in government debt and interest payment ratios." (Source: https://www.axios.com/2025/05/16/moody-us-credit-rating, May 2025)
- Private credit deal: Blue Owl led a $1.4 billion loan backing Hg's roughly $6.4 billion acquisition of OneStream, with Goldman Sachs Alternatives, Golub Capital, HPS and Blackstone. (Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-12/private-credit-piles-into-onestream-debt-despite-software-pains, Feb 12, 2026)
- Private credit deal: Blackstone's Champions Group buyout drew more than $1 billion of private credit including a delayed-draw term loan, priced about 4.5 points over the benchmark; lenders not named. (Source: https://news.bgov.com/private-equity/blackstone-taps-private-credit-to-fund-champions-group-buyout, Mar 4, 2026)
