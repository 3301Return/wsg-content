## 1. The STAR Method for Finance Interviews: Examples That Actually Land

Old: `drafts/draft-star-method-finance-interviews-v1.md` (5,790 words). New: `drafts/draft-star-method-finance-interviews-v2.md`. Images: slug `star-method-finance-interviews`.

### Editorial plan

- **Reader:** a sophomore or junior with a bank HireVue or first round in the next few weeks who has heard of STAR and wants to give better answers.
- **Their question:** how do I actually structure a good STAR answer, and what does a good one sound like?
- **The answer in three sentences:** STAR is Situation, Task, Action, Result, and most of a 90-second answer should go to the Action. Prepare about five true stories, each with a number in the Result, and adapt them to the question. Here are four full answers that show it, the mistakes that usually sink answers, and how to handle the recorded version.
- **Explain fully:** the four parts and the time split; picking and building your five stories; four worked answers; the handful of fixes that change an answer the most; the HireVue mechanics the student actually faces.
- **Two lines each:** what STAR stands for and the variants (CAR, STARR); that banks publish their values and interviewers often listen for them.
- **Cut:** the structured-interview research (Sackett et al. validity figures) and its chart; the per-bank competency lists; the 19-question mapping table and its image; four of the eight sample answers; the "why this works" paragraph after each answer; the say/don't section; the 2018 eFinancialCareers HireVue history and the question-count/retake detail beyond one line; repeated MIT quotes; the closing "every fix in ten minutes" bold.
- **Sections (one purpose each):** intro; What does STAR stand for? (what it is + one line on variants); How long should a STAR answer be? (length only); How to split the 90 seconds (the four parts, the I rule moves to mistakes); Prepare about five stories (story types as a short list with two or three typical questions each, no table; the "banks publish their values" two lines live here); Four STAR answers (four answers, no after-notes except one short line where it adds something not in the answer); STAR in a HireVue (practical steps only); The mistakes that usually cost the most (we vs I, memorizing, framing other people, invented stories; Situation length and missing numbers are already covered above, so not repeated); What to do this week.
- **Length:** focused, about 3,000 to 3,500 words.

### Fix list

1. **Delete section "1. Banks score your answers against a rubric, so structure matters"** entirely, including the validity research, the validity image (02-interview-validity) and its source line, and the per-bank competency lists. Move two plain sentences into the stories section: most banks publish their values (Goldman, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley each have a public list) and an interviewer will often be listening for them, so skim the list for each bank and pick the story that fits. No research, no "rubric," no "scored against a list."
2. **"What does STAR stand for?":** keep the first paragraph, hedge its bold ("Most variants a bank teaches... keep the same three moves"). Cut the variant paragraph to one sentence (some banks use CAR or STARR; JPMorgan's handbook uses CAR; pick one and stick with it). Keep the JPMorgan handbook link.
3. **"How long should a STAR method answer be?":** keep the length answer and the 150-word figure. Move the MIT split sentence and bold out (it belongs to the next section). The MIT "not need to know every detail!" quote appears three times in v1; keep it once, in the time-split section.
4. **Time-split section (old section 2):** keep the four bolded parts, the snippets and the image. Renumber. Cut the two closing paragraphs ("Put together, the four snippets..." and "The 'I' rule runs through all four parts...") since the full answer and the mistakes section carry those points. Hedge "because it's the part the interviewer scores" → "because it's the part interviewers usually care most about."
5. **Five stories section (old section 3):** retitle with a hedge ("Five stories usually cover most questions" or "Prepare about five stories"). Hedge every absolute in it ("For banking, five is the number," "every question on the list is covered," the bold claiming the five stories "cover all 19"). Replace the 19-question table and the story-matrix image (03-story-bank-matrix) with a short bulleted list: the five story types, each with two or three example questions in one line. Keep the student-fund paragraph but cut it to two sentences (the point: a student fund, a Research Challenge team or a club budget usually gives you a number; one example with a link is enough). Add the two lines on bank values from fix 1. Keep the "two questions from the same row" tip, reworded for the list.
6. **Sample answers (old section 4):** keep four: failure (student fund, with the marked-up image 04), team member not pulling weight (Research Challenge), leadership (treasurer budget), data recommendation (coffee shop). Cut the other four (tight deadline, influenced without authority, difficult feedback, learn quickly). Delete the "why it works" paragraph after each answer; where one adds something the answer doesn't show, keep a single short sentence (at most two of the four). Update the section intro (four answers, not eight) and hedge its bold.
7. **HireVue section (old section 5):** keep the opening two sentences on Goldman and JPMorgan using HireVue as a first round, then one sentence on the usual format (often three to six questions, about 30 seconds to prepare, up to about two minutes to answer, retakes vary by firm, check the attempts screen). Cut the 2018 eFinancialCareers paragraph and the help-center paragraph beyond that one line, and their VERIFY tags. Keep the practical paragraph (four words on a notepad, 150 words, click done) and the practice-questions line, and hedge the narrator bold ("the STAR structure has to be automatic" → advice). Keep the three WSG links.
8. **Mistakes section (old section 6):** keep four: "we" vs "I" (absorb the I rule from fix 4), memorizing sentences, describing other people, invented stories. Delete the Situation-length and missing-number items (covered earlier). Delete the closing bold about ten minutes. Hedge the header ("The mistakes that usually cost the most") and the intro ("list the same failures").
9. **Delete the "Say this, don't say that" section** entirely.
10. **"What to do this week":** keep, trim to the steps, hedge the closing bold (no "cover every behavioral question").
11. **Intro and meta:** meta says four worked answers. The intro's last sentence matches the new sections. The third sentence ("so the structure has to be automatic by the time the question appears") becomes plain advice.
12. **Tone pass across the whole draft (rule 21):** hedge every sweeping claim about how banks or interviewers behave; plain facts stay plain.

## 2. Accenture Strategy Analyst Career Guide

Old: `drafts/draft-accenture-strategy-analyst-career-guide-v2.md` (5,551 words). New: `drafts/draft-accenture-strategy-analyst-career-guide-v3.md`. Images: slug `accenture-strategy-analyst-career-guide`.

### Editorial plan

- **Reader:** a sophomore or junior who has Accenture Strategy on a list beside MBB and tier 2 firms.
- **Their question:** what is the Strategy Analyst job, what does it pay at entry, how do I get in, and how does it compare with MBB?
- **The answer in three sentences:** It's the entry-level consulting seat in Accenture's Strategy practice, hired mostly through a 10-week Summer Analyst internship that converts to the Strategy Analyst Development Program. It usually pays about $90,000 base and roughly $100,000 in total cash, about a quarter below MBB, with similar hours and case prep. The main difference is that Accenture's strategy work often hands off to its own technology and operations teams.
- **Explain fully:** what the analyst actually does; the program and how to apply (the posting, track selection, timing); the interview process; what to show in the application; how it compares with MBB.
- **Two lines each:** pay beyond the first year (Consultant after two to three years, rising from there); 401(k), relocation and signing; the earlier programs for first- and second-years; Potentia; the NAELFY posting code.
- **Cut:** the pay-by-level table and chart down to Managing Director; restricted stock at Manager, promotion timing to MD, up-or-out; the career-level numbering trivia; most of the kitchen-cabinet case (keep about one page); the comparison table image that repeats the markdown table; the pay prose under the comparison table that repeats it; the say/don't section (its "why Accenture" answer moves into the application section; the two case callouts repeat the case); the repeated October 16 deadline (keep it in the recruiting steps and once in the prep timeline at most).
- **Length:** focused, about 3,000 to 3,500 words.

### Fix list
