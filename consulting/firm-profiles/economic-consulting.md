# Economic and Litigation Consulting

A separate consulting category that hires undergrads in real numbers and pays at or above MBB base. Economic consulting firms support litigation, regulatory proceedings, and antitrust matters with quantitative analysis. The work is closer to applied microeconomics or econometrics than to MBB-style case work. Undergrads with strong economics, math, or statistics backgrounds are the target candidate pool. WSG readers studying economics or quantitative social sciences should treat this category as the premier alternative to MBB, not as fallback.

## Analysis Group

- **Founded:** 1981, Bruce Stangle and Michael Koehn.
- **Headcount:** ~1,500 across the US and Europe.
- **Undergrad role title:** Analyst.
- **Strongest practice:** Antitrust litigation, healthcare and pharma litigation, intellectual property disputes, securities litigation, employment economics.
- **Comp:** At or slightly above MBB base for the analyst role. Bonuses can run materially higher in busy years.
- **Schools that place heaviest:** Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT, Stanford, Chicago, Penn, Berkeley, Williams, Amherst. Strong placement from Ivy + top-LAC econ majors.
- **Why WSG readers care:** Analysis Group is probably the most-recommended non-MBB option for econ-major undergrads at top schools. PhD-style work with consulting-comparable comp. Real exits to top econ PhD programs (Harvard, MIT, Stanford), top law schools (HLS, YLS, SLS), and finance.
- **Lifestyle:** Hours vary by case. Significantly less travel than MBB. Office-based work.

## Cornerstone Research

- **Founded:** 1989.
- **Headcount:** ~1,000 in 9 US offices.
- **Undergrad role title:** Analyst.
- **Strongest practice:** Securities litigation, antitrust, financial institutions, accounting and valuation disputes, intellectual property.
- **Comp:** At or slightly above MBB base.
- **Why WSG readers care:** Direct peer of Analysis Group. Closely affiliated with academic experts at top universities (Stanford GSB, HBS, Wharton). Heavy PhD-feeder reputation.
- **Lifestyle:** Office-based, less travel than MBB.

## NERA Economic Consulting

- **Founded:** 1961.
- **Owned by:** Oliver Wyman / Marsh McLennan.
- **Headcount:** ~700 globally.
- **Undergrad role title:** Research Associate.
- **Strongest practice:** Antitrust, energy regulation, securities and finance litigation, environmental economics, international arbitration.
- **Comp:** Within 5-10% of MBB base for the analyst role.
- **Why WSG readers care:** One of the oldest economic consulting firms. Strong international footprint. Pairs well with candidates targeting energy or regulatory work.

## Compass Lexecon

- **Founded:** 2008, merger of Compass and Lexecon.
- **Owned by:** FTI Consulting.
- **Headcount:** ~700.
- **Strongest practice:** Antitrust, securities, mergers and acquisitions, finance and damages.
- **Comp:** At MBB-comparable base.
- **Why WSG readers care:** Deep antitrust dealflow. Notable Booth/Chicago Law affiliations.

## Bates White

- **Founded:** 1999.
- **Headcount:** ~400.
- **Strongest practice:** Antitrust, energy, environmental, healthcare, financial services litigation.
- **Comp:** Competitive with MBB.
- **Why WSG readers care:** Smaller than Analysis Group / Cornerstone but at the same quality tier for econ-major undergrads. Strong PhD-feeder.

## Brattle Group

- **Founded:** 1990.
- **Strongest practice:** Energy, electricity markets, finance and accounting, antitrust.
- **Why WSG readers care:** The leading energy-litigation economic consulting firm. Specialist option for energy-focused candidates.

## Charles River Associates (CRA)

- **Founded:** 1965.
- **Headcount:** ~900.
- **Strongest practice:** Antitrust, litigation support, regulatory work, finance economics.
- **Why WSG readers care:** Older name, broader practice set. Less prestige separation from Analysis Group / Cornerstone at the analyst level.

## What "economic consulting" looks like day-to-day

- **Build economic models** for litigation and regulatory matters. Run regressions in Stata, R, or Python.
- **Support expert witness testimony.** Senior economists (often academic professors) give expert opinions; analysts do the underlying analysis.
- **Less travel than MBB.** Most work is office-based.
- **Slower pace per project.** Engagements can run 6-18 months. Less per-week pivoting than MBB.
- **Quantitative-heavy.** Strong econometrics or statistics background expected.

## Recruiting differences from MBB

- **Case interview replaced by economics-driven case + technical questions.** Expect questions on microeconomic principles (price elasticity, marginal cost, Nash equilibrium), regression interpretation, and quantitative reasoning.
- **GPA bar is comparable to MBB.** 3.7+ effective floor at targets. Strong econ majors with thesis work get a boost.
- **Recruiting timeline aligned with MBB cycles.** Junior summer internship is the standard entry path, with applications open in spring of sophomore year for some firms.

## Exit options

- **Top econ PhD programs** (Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Princeton, Chicago, Yale, Berkeley) at materially higher rates than from MBB. Analysis Group and Cornerstone are explicit econ-PhD feeders.
- **Top law schools** (HLS, YLS, SLS) — litigation work is direct legal-career exposure.
- **Investment banking and finance** with quantitative emphasis.
- **Quant research and trading** at hedge funds.
- **Tech (data science / economics roles at Meta, Amazon, Uber, Microsoft, Google).** Tech economist roles are a recent significant exit path.

## The honest selection note

If a WSG article is about econ-major-friendly post-grad options, this category deserves its own section, not a footnote in an MBB article. The case for Analysis Group or Cornerstone over an MBB seat is real for the right candidate profile: PhD-track economists or law-track candidates.
