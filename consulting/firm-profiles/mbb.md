# MBB Firm Profiles — McKinsey, BCG, Bain

The three firms most consulting candidates ask about. They are the prestige tier of management consulting and they hire the largest undergrad analyst classes in the industry. Compensation, training, exits, and case-interview culture rhyme across all three, but each firm has a distinct voice the candidate is expected to know before networking events. This file is the source of truth WSG articles draw from when discussing any one of them.

## McKinsey & Company

- **Founded:** 1926, James O. McKinsey, Chicago.
- **Undergrad entry-level role title:** Business Analyst (BA). Sometimes called "Junior Associate" at non-US offices. The Summer Analyst variant is the Business Analyst Intern (BAI).
- **2026 cycle BA Intern deadline:** March 29, 2026 (for Summer 2026 internship, Class of 2027 juniors). [VERIFY: confirm against McKinsey careers page before any article publish]
- **Class size estimate:** ~3,000 entry-level hires globally per year across BA and BAI roles. US class roughly 700-900.
- **GPA bar:** No official floor. Effective floor at target schools is 3.7+. At non-targets, 3.85+ with a serious distinguishing factor.
- **Schools that place heaviest:** Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Stanford, Penn (Wharton), Duke, MIT, Columbia, Chicago, Northwestern, plus the top public-state targets (Michigan-Ross, UVA, Berkeley-Haas). Strong presence at Dartmouth, Brown, Cornell.
- **Practice areas to know:** McKinsey Implementation (MI), McKinsey Digital (formerly MD), Corporate Finance, Operations, Marketing & Sales, Risk & Resilience, Sustainability, Public Sector, McKinsey Health Institute.
- **Notable McKinsey-specific recruiting features:**
  - **Solve game** is a required pre-interview gamified assessment. Replaces the older PST (Problem Solving Test). Outcomes are pass/fail; firm has not published threshold publicly.
  - **PEI (Personal Experience Interview)** runs in every behavioral round. Three dimensions: personal impact, entrepreneurial drive, inclusive leadership. The expectation is a single STAR-formatted story per dimension, ~3 minutes each.
  - **Interviewer-led case style.** Different from BCG/Bain's candidate-led style. McKinsey interviewers walk you through the case in pre-set steps and ask specific questions. Less freeform than Bain.
- **Comp (2026 estimates):** US BA base $112K, performance bonus 10-25%, signing bonus $5K, total all-in target ~$130-140K. BAI summer comp ~$2,300/week base + housing stipend. [VERIFY: comp figures annually]
- **Tenure pattern:** BAs typically stay 2-3 years, then either get sponsored MBA placement or exit to PE, tech (PM roles), startups, or PE-backed companies in operating roles.

## Boston Consulting Group (BCG)

- **Founded:** 1963, Bruce Henderson, Boston.
- **Undergrad entry-level role title:** Associate. Summer version is Summer Associate.
- **2026 cycle Summer Associate deadline:** Expected April 2026 (for Class of 2027 juniors). BCG historically runs two windows per cycle — early summer and early fall. [VERIFY: confirm against BCG careers page]
- **Class size estimate:** ~2,500 entry-level hires globally per year. US class ~600-800.
- **GPA bar:** Same effective floor as McKinsey — 3.7+ at targets, higher at non-targets.
- **Schools that place heaviest:** Same target list as McKinsey, with BCG having a noticeably strong pull at Princeton, Stanford GSB pre-MBA pipeline, Duke, Northwestern (Kellogg pre-MBA).
- **Practice areas to know:** Strategy, Operations, Technology Advantage, BCG X (digital build), BCG GAMMA (data science), People & Organization, Climate & Sustainability, Public Sector.
- **Notable BCG-specific recruiting features:**
  - **Casey** is BCG's chatbot-based pre-interview assessment. Tests case-solving in a text-based simulation.
  - **Candidate-led case style.** Closer to Bain. You drive the structure, identify drivers, and walk the interviewer through your thinking. Less prompting from the interviewer than at McKinsey.
  - **Brainteaser-style sub-questions** are more common at BCG than at the other two. Be ready to do quick mental math under time pressure.
- **Comp (2026 estimates):** US Associate base $112K, performance bonus 10-25%, signing $5K. Summer Associate comp parity with McKinsey BAI. [VERIFY]
- **Tenure pattern:** Same as McKinsey — 2-3 years, then MBA sponsorship or exit.

## Bain & Company

- **Founded:** 1973, Bill Bain (formerly BCG), Boston.
- **Undergrad entry-level role title:** Associate Consultant (AC). Summer version is Associate Consultant Intern (ACI).
- **2026 cycle ACI deadline:** July 19, 2026 (for Summer 2026 internship). Bain explicitly offers a spring and fall window. [VERIFY: confirm against Bain careers page]
- **Class size estimate:** ~2,000 entry-level hires globally per year. US class ~500-700.
- **GPA bar:** 3.7+ effective floor at targets.
- **Schools that place heaviest:** Same target list with Bain having traditionally strong roots at Harvard, Yale, Stanford, MIT, Williams, Princeton. Bain Cap PE is a major destination exit, so Bain attracts the candidate set with PE-on-the-radar.
- **Practice areas to know:** Strategy, Performance Improvement, Customer Strategy & Marketing, Mergers & Acquisitions, Organization, Private Equity Group (LBO diligence engagements with Bain Cap and external PE clients).
- **Notable Bain-specific recruiting features:**
  - **Bain Online Test (BOT)** or SOVA assessment in some offices. Different from McKinsey Solve and BCG Casey.
  - **Candidate-led case style.** Similar to BCG. Heavier emphasis on the synthesis / "so what" closing than at McKinsey.
  - **Cultural emphasis on collegiality.** "A Bainie never lets another Bainie fail" is a real internal phrase that shows up in interviewer behavior. Behaviorals test team-fit hard.
- **Comp (2026 estimates):** US AC base $112K, performance bonus 10-25%, signing $5K. ACI summer comp parity with the other two. [VERIFY]
- **Tenure pattern:** ACs typically stay 2-3 years, with Bain Cap and other PE seats among the most common exits.

## What the three firms share

- **Up-or-out culture.** A consultant either gets promoted or gets "counseled out." Promotion windows are typically 2 years.
- **MBA sponsorship.** All three sponsor a portion of their high-performers for MBA programs (HBS, GSB, Wharton, Booth) with a return-offer guarantee post-MBA.
- **Class-of-X recruiting.** Every cycle targets a specific graduation class. The "2026 cycle" for an undergrad summer internship targets Class of 2027 juniors interning Summer 2026 (apply A → intern Summer A → graduate Spring A+1, since these are junior-summer internships, not the sophomore-summer model used by BB IB).
- **Case + behavioral interview structure.** Three rounds typical. Round 1 is usually one case + one behavioral. Round 2 (sometimes called the "final round") is two cases + a partner conversation.
- **Affinity programs.** All three run undergraduate sophomore-summer leadership programs aimed at early identification of diverse candidates: McKinsey Insight, BCG Bridge to BCG, Bain Build (formerly Building Entrepreneurial Leaders).

## What separates them in practice

- **McKinsey** is the most public-sector / government-facing of the three. Strongest internal training program. Most rigid case structure. Often the most-traveled lifestyle.
- **BCG** has the strongest digital / data-science offering through BCG X and GAMMA. Most "intellectually freeform" in the case. Slightly more boutique-feel office cultures.
- **Bain** is the most PE-adjacent of the three. Strongest collegial / team-first culture. Often the most predictable lifestyle of the three (slightly less travel-heavy on average, though all three travel hard).
